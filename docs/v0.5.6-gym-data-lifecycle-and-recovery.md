# v0.5.6 Gym Data Lifecycle, Retention, and Recovery Policy

> **Provider note (2026-08-02):** The ownership, retention, and recovery
> objectives in this policy remain the pilot baseline. Its Supabase-specific
> provider assumptions are historical and are superseded by the Neon controls
> in `docs/v0.6.0-timestamps-backup-and-recovery.md`.

## Status and scope

This is the approved operational baseline for the two-site Gym pilot. It
defines product ownership and operating responsibilities; it is not legal
advice or a substitute for the customer contract, privacy notice, or a
jurisdiction-specific retention requirement. Before taking a real customer
live, the platform administrator must reconcile any stricter contractual or
legal requirement with this baseline and record the exception.

The policy applies to the planned `customers`, `sites`, `domains`,
`site_content_revisions`, `site_assets`, `leads`, `site_memberships`, and safe
operational-event records described in the [Gym-first platform
plan](./v0.5.6-gym-first-platform-plan.md).

## Ownership and responsibilities

| Role | Owns/does | Must not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer | Owns the business content, branding, approved public copy, third-party membership links, and business use of received leads | Access another customer's site, database, backups, or platform credentials |
| Platform administrator | Creates customer/site records, controls domain state, initiates exports, suspension, cancellation, deletion, and restore requests; records each action | Put database credentials in the browser or bypass the documented approval/audit path |
| Content editor (future) | Creates drafts for assigned sites only | Publish, alter domains, view leads, or delete assets unless separately assigned |
| Platform operations owner | Maintains backups, restore rehearsals, encrypted exports, storage lifecycle jobs, and the recovery runbook | Restore directly into production without first validating a non-production restore |
| Migration lead | Controls the Sheets-to-database cutover checklist and source-of-truth declaration | Leave permanent dual writes or delete the Sheets backup during the pilot |

`site_id` remains the operational ownership boundary. Every public content
revision, asset, lead, membership, cache key, and lifecycle action must be
scoped to it. `customer_id` describes the commercial relationship and can own
multiple sites; it does not replace `site_id` in tenant-scoped records.

## Site lifecycle

| State | Entered by | Public behaviour | Data and exit rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `draft` | Platform administrator | Not resolvable from a public hostname | Content may be imported and validated. Publish only after a valid revision, domain/subdomain record, and visual review exist. |
| `active` | Platform administrator | Resolved hostname renders only the selected published revision and accepts validated leads | Normal operation. New content is draft first, then published as a new immutable revision. |
| `suspended` | Platform administrator | Safe not-found/maintenance response; no lead acceptance or public content render | Reversible. Retain data and domains while the reason is investigated. Re-enable only after the recorded condition is cleared and cache/domain state is rechecked. |
| `cancelled` | Platform administrator after recorded customer request or contract end | Suspend first, remove public serving, and stop new lead delivery | Begin the 30-day export window. Customer domains are removed from the platform before deletion. |
| `deleted` | Platform administrator after the cancellation window and approval record | Never resolves | Production records and assets are purged; residual backup copies expire on their documented schedule. The original customer/site identifiers are not reused. |

Domain lifecycle is separate: `requested` -> `pending_verification` ->
`active`, with `failed` and `removed` as terminal/non-serving states. A
normalized hostname is globally unique while it exists. A pending, failed,
removed, disabled, or duplicate domain never falls back to a different site.

## Retention and deletion schedule

These are deliberate pilot defaults. A stricter customer contract or legal
hold wins and must be recorded before its normal deletion deadline.

| Data | Normal retention | Deletion responsibility | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Published and superseded content revisions | While the site is active, plus 90 days after supersession or cancellation | Platform administrator through a scheduled, audited job | Keep enough history for a customer-approved rollback and migration comparison. |
| Draft content | 30 days after the last change, unless it is published | Platform administrator | Avoid accumulating abandoned imports and sensitive draft copy. |
| Site assets and image derivatives | Until no retained revision references the asset, then 30 days | Platform operations owner | Delete original, derived, and cache-addressable objects by the server-generated `site_id`/asset key. |
| Leads | 12 months from receipt, unless the customer requests an earlier allowed deletion or a documented obligation requires longer | Platform administrator | Leads contain contact data; exports are site-scoped and access is logged. Do not keep content indefinitely "just in case." |
| Safe operational events | 90 days | Platform operations owner | Store only safe metadata such as site ID, event, status, duration, and request ID; never lead messages, credentials, or tokens. |
| Customer/site record after cancellation | 30-day export window, then delete/anonymise operational fields not required for audit | Platform administrator | Preserve a minimal non-personal deletion audit record: site ID hash/reference, deletion time, approver, and result. |
| Database backups and encrypted logical exports | Provider daily backups for 7 days; one encrypted weekly logical export retained for 35 days | Platform operations owner | Backup expiry is not immediate deletion from a historical backup. Do not restore deleted customer data unless an approved recovery/hold requires it. |

The customer export window is 30 calendar days from the recorded cancellation
notice. During it, the platform administrator can provide a site-scoped
content export and lead export through a controlled server-side process. The
export must never contain another site's rows, credentials, or internal audit
notes. Do not email raw database dumps or put them in a public download URL.

## Backup and recovery baseline

### Chosen pilot recovery objective

- **Recovery point objective (RPO):** up to 24 hours of recently written
  database data may be lost in a provider-wide database disaster during the
  pilot. This follows from daily database backups.
- **Recovery time objective (RTO):** restore the database to a non-production
  validation environment and decide whether production recovery is safe within
  four business hours of declaring an incident. The actual provider restore
  duration is measured in the rehearsal; it is not assumed.
- **No paid point-in-time recovery for the pilot by default.** It is a scale or
  risk escalation option, not a hidden requirement. Revisit it immediately if
  the business cannot accept a 24-hour RPO, or if a customer contract requires
  a shorter recovery window.

The current recommended provider starting point is Supabase/PostgreSQL. Its
current documentation says Pro projects receive daily backups retained for 7
days and that storage objects are not restored by a database backup alone.
Therefore the pilot must also maintain an asset inventory/export and test the
asset restore path, not only the database restore. Supabase's current
point-in-time-recovery add-on starts at about USD 100/month for seven days, so
it is excluded from the low-cost pilot estimate unless the RPO changes.

### Backup responsibilities

1. The platform operations owner verifies the managed-database daily-backup
   status each week and records the oldest restorable point.
2. Once per week, the operations owner creates an encrypted logical database
   export and an asset manifest in a controlled, non-public backup location.
   The export is retained for 35 days and access is limited to recovery staff.
3. Before every migration/cutover, the migration lead takes an immutable Gym
   Sheets export and records its checksum, row counts, and timestamp. The
   source Sheet remains read-only fallback/rollback evidence until the pilot
   retirement gate approves removal.
4. The platform administrator records any customer deletion request, export,
   suspension, cancellation, and completed deletion in an audit log that does
   not contain lead message text or secrets.

### Recovery runbook

1. **Declare and contain.** The platform administrator sets the affected site
   to `suspended`, stops public serving/lead writes for that site, preserves
   evidence, and records the incident/request ID.
2. **Choose recovery point.** The operations owner selects the latest safe
   provider backup or weekly export that predates the incident. Do not restore
   in place first.
3. **Restore safely.** Restore into a separate non-production project or
   database. Verify database schema, row counts by `site_id`, published
   revisions, lead counts, domain mappings, asset manifest, and that site A
   cannot see site B.
4. **Approve production action.** The migration lead and platform administrator
   compare the restore to the recovery objective and decide whether to repair,
   restore production, or roll back to the last published content revision.
5. **Recover and verify.** Perform the approved production action, invalidate
   only the affected site's cache namespaces, test the canonical hostname and
   lead path without submitting a real lead, then return the site to `active`.
6. **Close and improve.** Record the actual RPO/RTO, data gap, decisions, and
   follow-up work. Repeat the rehearsal at least once before the pilot launch
   and after any database/storage-provider change.

## Deletion checklist

Deletion is not a single `DELETE` command. The platform administrator must
confirm all of the following before marking a site deleted:

- Cancellation date, approving actor, and 30-day export window are recorded.
- The customer has received or declined the site-scoped export.
- Public domain records are removed/disabled and the hostname resolves safely.
- The site's published content, drafts, leads, memberships, assets, and cache
  namespaces are deleted or scheduled in the correct dependency order.
- Asset originals and derivatives are removed after retained revisions no
  longer reference them.
- The backup-expiry date and the fact that immediate backup purge is not
  promised are communicated in the deletion record.
- A minimal non-personal audit result remains, and no active task can restore
  or publish the deleted site without a new customer/site onboarding flow.
